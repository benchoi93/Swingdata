### 3. Distributional TWFE DiD Exploration

Designed and implemented a distributional extension of the TWFE DiD. Instead of testing only mean effects, we compute city-month quantile summaries (p25, p50, p75, p90) and run TWFE DiD at each quantile. **Note:** This is a distributional TWFE DiD on quantile summaries, not a formal unconditional quantile treatment effect estimator (Firpo, Fortin, Lemieux 2009). A formal RIF-regression approach would require individual-level panel data.

**Script:** `src/v2/qte_exploration.py`
**Data:** `data_parquet/v2/city_month_quantile_panel.parquet` (549 obs, 52 cities x 11 months)
**Figure:** `figures/v2/fig_qte_exploration.pdf`

**Key Findings:**

| Outcome | Mean beta | p25 beta | p50 beta | p75 beta | p90 beta | Tail ratio |
|---------|-----------|----------|----------|----------|----------|------------|
| Mean speed | -1.44* | -0.75 ns | -1.22* | -1.93*** | -3.08*** | 2.15x |
| Cruise fraction | +0.04 ns | -0.00 ns | +0.02 ns | +0.04 ns | +0.36*** | 8.44x |
| Speed CV | -0.00 ns | -0.02 ns | -0.01 ns | -0.00 ns | +0.02 ns | — |
| Zero-speed frac | +0.01 ns | -0.00 ns | +0.00 ns | +0.01 ns | +0.02 ns | — |
| Harsh accel (mean) | -0.16*** | — | — | -1.25* | -0.18 ns | — |
| Harsh decel (mean) | -0.19*** | — | — | +0.65 ns | -1.29** | — |

**Interpretation:**
- **Mean speed** shows strong distributional heterogeneity: the TUB ban reduces p90 speed by 3.08 km/h (p<1e-16) but p25 by only 0.75 km/h (ns). Speed governors disproportionately reduce extreme speeds (tail ratio = 2.15x).
- **Cruise fraction** reveals a hidden tail effect: while the mean is null, the 90th percentile shows a +0.36 increase (p<1e-7). The TUB ban forces high-speed trips into governed modes, dramatically increasing cruising at the right tail.
- **Speed CV** and **zero-speed fraction** are null at all quantiles, confirming the mean-level finding.
- **Harsh events** are difficult to analyze at quantiles due to zero-inflation (p25 and p50 are all zeros).

**Research potential:** The mean speed and cruise fraction results provide a more nuanced policy message: speed governors disproportionately reduce extreme risk rather than shifting the entire distribution. This is publishable as either a standalone note or a supplementary analysis in the AMAR paper.
