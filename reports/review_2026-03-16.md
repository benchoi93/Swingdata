### Suggested Research Directions

1. **Quantile Treatment Effects of the TUB Ban** (Feasibility: HIGH)
   - Method: Estimate unconditional quantile treatment effects (Firpo, Fortin, and Lemieux 2009 RIF-regression) of the Dec 2023 TUB ban on the full distribution of harsh event counts and speed CV, not just the mean. Use the same city-month panel as the current TWFE DiD but replace the conditional mean with quantile-specific estimands. Compare effects at the 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentiles of each outcome.
   - Data needed: city_month_panel.parquet (already exists). Trip-level distributions can be computed from routes_all.parquet. No external data required.
   - Expected finding: The TUB ban likely has heterogeneous effects across the outcome distribution. The strongest safety improvements may occur at the right tail (high-risk trips with many harsh events), while the median trip is largely unaffected. This would show that speed governors disproportionately reduce extreme risk rather than shifting the entire distribution -- a more nuanced policy message than the current mean-based DiD.
   - Target venue: AMAR (quantile methods are methodologically novel for micromobility) or Journal of Econometrics applied section
   - Connection to current work: Directly extends the TWFE DiD (Section 4.4) from mean effects to distributional effects. The current paper already computes distributional shape metrics (skewness, kurtosis) but treats them as outcomes rather than analyzing the treatment effect distribution itself.

2. **Staggered Deployment Natural Experiment via City-Level Entry Timing** (Feasibility: MEDIUM)
   - Method: Swing likely expanded to different cities at different times during 2022-2023. Use the 24-month panel to identify staggered city entry dates. Apply the Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) heterogeneity-robust DiD estimator to estimate city-cohort-specific treatment effects of Swing deployment on local riding behavior. Instrument for mode availability using city entry timing.
   - Data needed: routes_all.parquet (44.1M trips, 24 months). City-level first-trip dates can identify deployment timing. The 2022 data (pre-speed-data) still has trip counts and distances for demand analysis.
   - Expected finding: Cities where Swing deployed later may show different baseline speeding patterns due to selection effects. The staggered design can separate deployment effects from city fixed effects, providing a second causal identification strategy beyond the TUB ban.
   - Target venue: Transportation Science or TR Part A (policy evaluation focus)
   - Connection to current work: The current paper uses only the Dec 2023 TUB ban as a natural experiment. A second natural experiment (staggered deployment) would provide complementary causal evidence and address the single-shock limitation noted in the Limitations section.

3. **Conditional Diffusion Models for Privacy-Preserving Synthetic Speed Profiles** (Feasibility: MEDIUM)
   - Method: Train a conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) on within-trip speed sequences, conditioned on trip metadata (mode, distance, road class, time of day). Generate synthetic but realistic speed profiles. Evaluate fidelity via distributional metrics (MMD, Frechet distance on speed feature space) and utility preservation (downstream speeding prediction AUC on synthetic vs. real data). Compare against TimeGAN and basic noise-addition baselines.
   - Data needed: Raw speed vectors from routes_all.parquet (44.1M trips). Trip metadata already available. Training on a 1M-trip subsample with GPU is computationally feasible.
   - Expected finding: Diffusion models should capture the bimodal speed distributions (governed vs. ungoverned), temporal structure (acceleration-cruise-deceleration pattern), and mode-specific dynamics. The synthetic data should preserve enough signal for speeding prediction (AUC within 0.02 of real data) while providing formal privacy guarantees. This enables open sharing of this proprietary dataset.
   - Target venue: NeurIPS or ICML workshop on generative models for science, or TR Part C (aligned with PI generative models tutorial paper, Choi et al. 2025)
   - Connection to current work: Directly leverages the PI expertise in deep generative models (TrajGAIL, generative tutorial in TR Part C 2025). The current paper documents behavioral differences between modes that the generative model must reproduce. The privacy-preserving angle addresses the data availability limitation noted in the current paper.
