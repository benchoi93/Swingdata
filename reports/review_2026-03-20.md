### Suggested Research Directions

1. **Deep Learning on Within-Trip Speed Sequences** (Feasibility: HIGH)
   - Method: Train 1D-CNN or Transformer encoder on raw per-trip speed vectors (variable-length time series at ~10s intervals) for binary classification (safe vs. risky trip, defined by exceeding 25 km/h or having harsh events). Compare against the current LightGBM (AUC=0.905) which uses hand-crafted features. Use attention maps to identify which trip segments drive risk.
   - Data needed: speeds column from routes_all.parquet (already available as variable-length arrays); speeding labels from trip_indicators.parquet. No new data collection needed.
   - Expected finding: Sequence models should match or slightly exceed LightGBM AUC while providing temporal interpretability (e.g., risk concentrates in first 60s or last 30s of trips). Attention patterns may reveal that acceleration phases at trip start are most predictive.
   - Target venue: TR Part C or NeurIPS Workshop on AI for Transportation
   - Connection to current work: Extends Phase 8 SHAP analysis (which showed TUB mode dominates) by asking whether the raw speed signal contains information beyond mode labels. If sequence models improve only marginally, it validates that mode is the sufficient statistic for risk -- a strong policy conclusion.

2. **Causal Effect Heterogeneity via Quantile DiD** (Feasibility: HIGH)
   - Method: Apply Callaway and Li (2019) quantile difference-in-differences to the Dec 2023 TUB ban, estimating treatment effects at the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentiles of each safety outcome (speed CV, harsh events, cruise fraction). This extends the current TWFE which estimates only the conditional mean effect. The distributional TWFE appendix already exists but uses QTE on a different estimand.
   - Data needed: city_month_panel.parquet with quantile-level aggregates (needs computation from trip_modeling.parquet, but straightforward).
   - Expected finding: Treatment effects likely concentrate in the upper tail -- cities with the worst pre-ban safety profiles see the largest improvements. This would support targeted enforcement rather than blanket bans.
   - Target venue: Transportation Science or AMAR
   - Connection to current work: Directly extends the TWFE DiD (Block 2) from mean to distributional effects. The current paper shows the average city saw a 28pp speeding reduction; quantile DiD would reveal whether this masks heterogeneity (e.g., some cities saw 5pp, others 50pp).

3. **User Behavioral Trajectory Modeling with Hidden Markov Models** (Feasibility: MEDIUM)
   - Method: For users with 50+ trips (substantial subset of 1.82M users), fit a Hidden Markov Model with 3-4 latent states (e.g., cautious, normal, risk-seeking, habitual speeder). Emission distributions are the 6 behavioral dimensions per trip. Estimate transition matrices to identify: (a) what fraction of users transition from cautious to risk-seeking over time, (b) whether mode switching (TUB adoption) coincides with state transitions, (c) whether state transitions are reversible.
   - Data needed: user_longitudinal.parquet + trip_experience.parquet (both available). Need to extract per-trip behavioral vectors for each user sequence.
   - Expected finding: Most users remain in their initial state (stable behavioral type), but 15-20% transition to riskier states, primarily mediated by TUB mode adoption. This would extend the current experience analysis (3.8% to 28.0% speeding increase) by revealing that the aggregate trend masks two subpopulations: stable safe riders and escalating risk-takers.
   - Target venue: TR Part C or KDD (Applied Data Science track)
   - Connection to current work: Extends Phase 8.1 (experience/escalation) and Phase 4 (compensation/substitution) by modeling individual trajectories rather than population averages. The GEE log(trip_rank) OR=1.178 shows an average escalation trend; HMM would decompose this into latent behavioral types and their transition dynamics.
